The Instagram username rules (2026)

Before picking a username, know what Instagram allows:

  • Length: 1 to 30 characters. The sweet spot for memorability is 8 to 15 characters.
  • Characters: Letters, numbers, periods, and underscores. No spaces, no hyphens, no special characters.
  • Uniqueness: Globally unique. If you want a name, you have to claim it before someone else does.
  • Periods and underscores don't make a name unique. jane.doe, jane_doe, and janedoe are all considered the same handle by Instagram's system.
  • Changes have a 14-day cooldown. Don't pick a name you'll regret. You can change it, but not often.

How real Instagram handles are named (the 6 patterns)

After looking at thousands of real Instagram accounts, the patterns collapse to six templates:

  1. First name + activity or niche word - cookingwithsam, miaskincare, jessrunssf. The most common creator pattern. Uses your first name so fans can find you, plus a keyword that signals your content.
  2. Name + small meaningful number - liv_04, maya.22, max_95. Personal accounts do this. The number is usually a birth year, lucky number, or just a number they like. Avoid random number strings like emma57831 - they look like a bot.
  3. First name + last initial - jessica.r, sarah.m, luke.k. Personal accounts where you want your real name but the full last name is taken.
  4. the / hey / its / by / get / try + name - thebrandname, heymia, itsmax, bynora, getgrowth, trycreate. Brand and creator accounts. Reads as a voice.
  5. Mood + aesthetic object - softbloom, velvetdawn, goldenhour, quietcloud. Aesthetic, lifestyle, photography, art. No first name. Just a feeling and a thing.
  6. One short, hard word - vex, nyx, flux, kai, onyx, ember. The minimalist move. Four to six letters. The hardest to get because everyone wants them.

How to check if an Instagram username is available

Two ways. The fast way: open Instagram's sign-up screen, type the handle, and watch for the green check or the red "this username isn't available" message. The slower way: search for the exact handle in the Instagram search bar. If a profile comes up, it's taken. If you see "No results", it might still be available, or it might be hidden behind an inactive account.

For inactive accounts, there's no public way to claim them. Some users wait 2 to 5 years and then contact Instagram support to ask for username release, but Instagram doesn't have a public process for this. The honest answer: if the name is taken by a dead account, you'll probably have to pick a different name.

Should your Instagram handle match your TikTok and YouTube?

Yes, in 99% of cases. A consistent handle across platforms makes it dramatically easier for new fans to find you everywhere. Imagine someone watches your TikTok, types your handle into Instagram, and finds you instantly. The reverse is also true. If your handle is different on every platform, you lose some of those searches to people who gave up and followed a competitor instead.

The exception: if your Instagram handle is taken but the same name is free on YouTube, you have three options. (1) Pick a close variation everywhere, like @cookingwithsam on YouTube and @samcooks on Instagram. (2) Use a different handle on Instagram and add a "link in bio" to your YouTube on the Instagram profile. (3) Pick a completely new name that is free everywhere, and lose the older audience for the sake of consistency.

Display name vs. username: what's the difference?

Your username is the unique handle that appears in your profile URL and in mentions. It's the part after the @ and it's globally unique on Instagram. Your display name is the readable name shown at the top of your profile, next to your posts in the feed, and in your comments. It doesn't need to be unique and you can change it any time.

A common pattern: use a short, brandable handle (@cookingwithsam) and a longer, descriptive display name (Sam | Sourdough & slow cooking). The handle is for tagging and searching, the display name is for first impressions.

Names to avoid on Instagram

  • Numbers that look like bot strings: @emma57831 looks like a bot or a default account. Use a meaningful number instead: birth year, lucky number, single digit.
  • Underscores at the start or end: Looks like a placeholder. _velvet is a worse choice than @velvet.
  • Long compound words without separators: @ilovefashionforever is hard to read. @ilovefashionforever doesn't help. Use periods: @i.love.fashion.
  • Brand names you don't own: If your handle is @nike_fan_official, you're one trademark complaint away from losing it. Don't use brand names.
  • Hard-to-spell words: If you have to spell it out loud three times, it's not the right name. The easier you are to find, the more followers you'll get from word-of-mouth.

Frequently asked questions

How long can an Instagram username be?

Instagram usernames can be 2 to 30 characters. The sweet spot for memorability and shareability is 8 to 15 characters. Shorter is better, but don't sacrifice meaning to save a character.

Can Instagram usernames have periods and underscores?

Yes, both. But Instagram treats jane.doe, jane_doe, and janedoe as the same handle, so you can't claim multiple variations of the same name. Pick one separator and stick with it. Most modern Instagram handles use periods, since they read softer than underscores.

How often can I change my Instagram username?

You can change your Instagram username, but there's a 14-day cooldown between changes. So don't pick a name you'll regret. Also note that your old username becomes available for someone else to claim the moment you change it.

What if my Instagram handle is taken on a dead account?

There's no public process to claim a handle from an inactive account. You can report the account to Instagram for impersonation if it's actually pretending to be you or your brand, but if it's just a dormant account that happens to have your name, you'll need to pick a different name.

Should I use my real name on Instagram?

For personal accounts, yes. Your real name (or a close variation like first initial + last name) is the most discoverable choice. For creator or brand accounts, use a niche + first name handle so the name communicates your content at a glance (e.g. cookingwithsam for a food channel).
